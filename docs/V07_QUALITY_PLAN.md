# Quality priorities after the actual V0.7 evaluation

Based on 36 new H100 generations and six reused synthetic outputs. These are AI visual findings on three licensed real corner/room views, not an independent human benchmark. See the execution report (`../ROOMCRAFT_V07_REPORT.md`, local research artifact excluded from the source candidate) and all pair reviews (`../experiments/v07_visual_review.json`, local research artifact excluded from the source candidate).

1. **Use native 1024 for final quality review, 768 for previews.** Both worked without quantization/offload. Real-photo generation averaged about 33/39 seconds at 1024 for base/adapter, versus 26/31 at 768. Native crops show useful textile detail; 1024 is not universally aesthetically better and changes furniture/noise. Do not upscale old 512 results and label them native.
2. **Retain Qwen and keep the adapter as an experimental preservation option.** The adapter retains window-room framing more closely at 768 across two seeds; apartment low-resolution framing also improves. Unchanged Qwen is much better at native 1024 than its earlier 512 failures suggest. Hotel quality is comparable. There is no evidence here requiring a foundation-model replacement or showing universal adapter superiority.
3. **Get owner/blinded human review on a broader authorized real-photo holdout before another training run.** Include full living rooms, bedrooms and dining rooms, multiple visible doors/windows, varied lenses, dark/bright interiors and awkward circulation. Current three views do not support commercial reliability claims. Preserve these evaluation images as holdouts. Use the existing per-pair review sheets; retain failures and judge geometry separately from furniture appeal.
4. **For the next approved adapter training experiment, prioritize separate rights-cleared real paired data and instruction compliance.** Obtain fixed-camera empty/furnished photographs with explicit commercial ML/adapter rights and property/photographer permissions. Use property-disjoint splits. Target observed missing dresser, moved/added outlet, ceiling-feature drift, lighting-control and furniture-placement failures. A small staged real-photo adaptation is a testable hypothesis, not a proven improvement. Do not train on these evaluation photos.
5. **Improve synthetic diversity/appearance selectively.** Higher-resolution inference did not fix smooth source walls/floors or plain window exteriors. Compare a small sample of richer physically scaled textures, realistic exteriors, lighting variations and denoising/sample settings before regenerating many scenes. More nearly identical renders are lower priority than real-photo validation. No gross synthetic-style collapse was observed on the three real photos, so do not presume domain shift is the primary remaining defect.
6. **Test higher-resolution training only against a controlled baseline.** Native 1024 inference already produces convincing detail from the 512-trained adapter. A later 768/1024 training pilot may help, but the current evidence does not justify claiming it is necessary or sufficient. Keep matched prompts/seeds and held-out properties, with a base-versus-current-versus-new adapter comparison.
7. **Add architecture-specific conditioning/loss after collecting annotations for actual failures.** Evaluate protected window/door/ceiling/outlet regions; account for legitimate furniture occlusion, shadows and reflections. Use structure-aware losses/conditioning only if broader real holdouts show persistent drift. Whole-image similarity to the empty input is not a furniture-quality score. Avoid hard compositing that reintroduces the V0.2 lighting seams without testing it.

No training, publication or new resource authorization is implied by this plan. Strict pixel-level material/lighting preservation, room-clearance safety and general commercial readiness remain unproven.
